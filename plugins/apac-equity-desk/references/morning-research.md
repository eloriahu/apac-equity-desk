# Researching the morning note

The note should explain what has changed for this market since its previous
close, which local exposures matter, where the first explanation may be wrong,
and what could change the assessment today. The published format is an editorial
choice made after investigation. More paragraphs or more links alone do not
establish depth.

## Discover the agenda

Establish the requested morning cutoff and the previous local session before
searching. Separate event time, publication/update time and quote time. A new
article about an old event is not a fresh catalyst; a Monday morning can require
the whole weekend. A retrospective note must not use later announcements or
closing moves to explain an earlier opening.

For a live note, recheck material quote and event changes before delivery when
the investigation has outlasted the initial snapshot. Keep the actual data cutoff
visible. This refresh does not apply to an explicitly historical cutoff.

Use the supplied Bloomberg tape as observations and leads, not a complete news
agenda. Unless the user limits sources, actively search current company/exchange
disclosures, domestic policy and macro releases, overnight global developments,
credible local reporting and the relevant event calendar. Use native-language
issuer names and official local sources when translations or English summaries
leave a material ambiguity. Open the underlying material for important claims;
search snippets, copied headlines and syndicated repetitions are discovery aids.
Do not imply access to a feed, paid report or consensus series that is unavailable.

Look across index divergence and concentration, sectors and exceptions, FX/rates
and relevant commodities, and company earnings/guidance/capital events. These are
search directions, not mandatory prose sections. Investigate domestic stories
even when the first international recap supplies a convenient US-led narrative.
Prioritize by likely local impact, surprise, timing, exposure and uncertainty;
give a dominant story more attention than a quiet category.

## Let findings drive the next search

Turn each material observation into a question whose answer could change the
note. Choose the next source from that question rather than repeating a generic
market-news search. For example:

- A semiconductor rally invites a check of the actual earnings or capex release:
  what changed versus prior guidance or a dated consensus, which local suppliers
  have demonstrated exposure, and which peers failed to follow?
- A currency move invites a check of the policy wording, rate path and intraday
  reversal, then revenue translation, input costs or margins for the named local
  names. An intraday yield high cannot stand in for the closing yield.
- An index rally with weak breadth invites a contribution check. Without weights
  and contributions, distinguish visible heavyweight moves from a proved claim
  that those stocks account for most of the index return.
- A stock moving against its sector invites company filings, guidance, customer
  exposure and valuation/positioning alternatives. Test a stock-specific cause
  before assuming that the sector theme explains it.

For the stories that drive the morning view, establish the fresh fact and its
timing, the economic mechanism, the named local exposure, relative-market fit
and the strongest counterevidence or alternative. Quantify the underlying
change where supported: guidance versus prior guidance, actual versus consensus,
rate/FX changes, or stock versus benchmark/peers. Keep estimates and analyst
inferences separate from reported facts. Do not manufacture an earnings
sensitivity, consensus surprise or valuation argument without the inputs.

Follow consequential links into customers, suppliers, domestic policy, earnings
and cross-assets when they could change the answer. This work belongs within
morning-brief; it does not need a new user instruction to invoke another skill.
Refresh the hypothesis when a later or more authoritative source contradicts it.
Checking additional websites that all repeat one wire does not resolve a gap.

## Keep a working record, not a checklist score

Alongside quotes and the claim/source registry, keep brief working notes for the
material questions: why each matters, sources actually inspected and their
cutoffs, the finding, competing evidence, the next useful check, and why the
question is resolved or remains limited. Record missing coverage and failed
access separately from a search that found no material update. Do not mark a
source as checked merely because its URL appeared in search results.

This is an internal research convention, not a new required JSON schema or
reader-facing table. `pack_readiness.py` checks field completeness and supplied
freshness; `fact_check.py` cannot assess the quality of the investigation. A
`ready` pack or a populated `mechanism` field is not proof that the source supports
the explanation.

## Decide when the work is sufficient

Continue when a feasible source check could materially change the lead, a named
exposure, the causal assessment or a near-term catalyst. In particular, pursue
an accessible primary release behind a decisive headline, an untested conflicting
observation, or a gap raised by verification. Filling every output field,
reaching the word guide or finding the first plausible explanation is not a
stopping rule. There is no fixed search or source-count quota.

Finish when the important developments have been investigated, the main
explanations tested, and further accessible checks are unlikely to change the
morning view. A quiet day can produce a shorter note. If material uncertainty
survives targeted checks, preserve it: state what was checked, the best-supported
alternatives, the unavailable evidence and the dated event or observable outcome
that would discriminate. `No single catalyst is confirmed` is a valid finding
after investigation, not a substitute for it. Do not promise a follow-up run.

Honor an explicit time budget or supplied-sources-only request. If access fails
or time runs out, deliver only the supported scope with the research boundary
visible. Do not ask for more inputs when available public research can answer the
question; ask only for essential inaccessible material. An absent live quote feed
must not become an invented opening tape or an excuse to skip accessible news
research. If that absence blocks a market note, return the supported news findings
and the specific quote gap without claiming a complete morning note.

## Write the view earned by the research

Lead an open-session note with the local tape and the most important interpretation;
lead a pre-open note with the setup and conditional local implications. Develop
the decisive overnight and domestic stories, named exposures and meaningful
exceptions in connected prose. Explain what is new and what appears already
reflected in prices only where comparable evidence supports that judgment.

End with the selective catalysts or observable tests that matter to this view:
verified date/local time, relevant expectation when sourced, and what outcome
would strengthen or weaken the assessment. A generic list of calendar events
adds little. Preserve counterevidence and uncertainty during editing. Keep the
research log and workflow mechanics out of the client note; expose only material
coverage limits in review notes. Use the house-style length guide flexibly, and
compress only when requested or when the day's evidence needs less space.
