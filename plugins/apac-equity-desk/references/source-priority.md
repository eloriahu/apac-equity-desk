# Source hierarchy and evidence language

Rank the underlying evidence, not the website that repeated it. A syndicated story and three reposts are one source.

## Level 1 — primary / authoritative

Company announcements and direct statements; exchange filings; regulator or government releases; official economic data; court records; audited reports; official transaction documents.

Language: `confirmed`, `reported by the company`, `the filing shows`. Check time, scope and whether the item is new.

## Level 2 — established reporting / attributable research

Reuters, Bloomberg and other established financial media with original reporting; attributable broker/analyst research; direct interviews where the speaker is named.

Language: `reported`, `according to`, `analysts at … estimate`. Do not upgrade a reported proposal into an enacted decision.

## Level 3 — specialist / industry evidence

SMM and other specialist price/data services; industry associations; reputable trade publications; channel checks with a disclosed method.

Language: `industry data indicate`, `a specialist survey suggests`. Describe methodology limitations and distinguish estimates from reported company data.

## Level 4 — unverified / social

Eastmoney forums, Xueqiu, Weibo, Telegram, anonymous posts, unattributed screenshots and market chatter.

Language: `unconfirmed chatter circulating on … claimed …`; add `the company has/has not responded` when known. Never write the claim as a causal fact. A price move coinciding with chatter is not proof of causation.

## Evidence ranking

Score a proposed driver on four dimensions, each 0–2:

- authority: Level 1 = 2, Level 2/3 = 1, Level 4 = 0
- freshness: first disclosed this session = 2, newly updated = 1, stale/unknown = 0
- specificity: directly names the security/event = 2, sector read-through = 1, broad macro = 0
- market fit: timing and relative-price pattern strongly fit = 2, partially fit = 1, contradict = 0

`7–8` high confidence, `4–6` medium, `0–3` low. This is an editorial confidence score, not a statistical probability.

## Conflict handling

Record the conflicting observations, their timestamps and definitions. Prefer primary evidence but do not silently discard a credible conflict. Common causes include delayed quotes, different close definitions, local versus consolidated volume, FX timestamps, revised data and duplicate news.
