# Research from supplied market data

Apply this when a research request includes screenshots, pasted prices or tables,
exports, headline panels or a current-task data pack. Use the supplied observations
immediately, then research the explanation. `Use the screenshot data` selects
the market observations; only an explicit restriction such as `use only these
sources` limits the evidence universe. Extraction-only and edit-only requests
retain their narrower scope.

## Accept the input directly

Read attached or pasted material with available image/file tools. Reuse the
active task's subject and deliverable; do not ask the user to retype readable
rows, prepare JSON, choose skills or separately request a news search. Ask only
when an essential identity or task cannot be resolved. Do not invent a research
task from an unexplained attachment without relevant context.

Record source kind, artifact/message identity, provider when known, visible data
time, task time, timezone, movement basis and coverage. Transcribe numerical
signs, units and values rather than screen colours. Keep cropped/ambiguous cells
missing and check company/listing labels before attaching news. A supplied daily
percentage is usable without last and previous-close prices; do not invent those
prices to satisfy a calculation helper.

Use the supplied observations as the quote baseline. Prefer supplied Bloomberg
observations when sources overlap; retain conflicts and field lineage under
`integrations.md`. Fill necessary missing/stale fields from available sources
rather than recollecting the entire screen. Preserve the original values when
adding a newer observation and distinguish the two cutoffs.

An upload time is not a quote time. Without a visible timestamp, label the values
as a supplied snapshot with unknown observation time, not verified live prices
or closes. This still permits bounded analysis. A watchlist does not establish
exchange-wide breadth, and co-movement alone does not establish flows or rotation.
Keep private artifacts and prior chat excerpts out of fixtures.

Declare timing/coverage gaps in the pack. Do not bypass a helper's live-data gate:
topic-radar requires timestamped quotes for live ranking. If that gate blocks,
offer only the bounded snapshot analysis that the evidence supports, clearly
separate from a live ranking; never fabricate fields or claim readiness passed.

## Investigate beyond the screen

Use the observations to choose material questions, not to preselect the answer.
Check relevant company/exchange disclosures, established reporting,
overnight/global developments, policy and macro, and peer/supply-chain evidence.
Open underlying sources for material claims. A supplied news panel is a set of
leads: verify its timing and reach, and look for consequential omitted news.
Follow findings that could change the lead rather than stopping at a generic
market recap or the first matching headline.

Separate three questions:

- **Existing backdrop:** Was the group already rising or falling before the
  latest event? Look back far enough to establish the trend and earlier drivers.
- **Fresh change:** What new information arrived in the session or overnight,
  when was it first public, and did it precede the observed move?
- **Exposure and exceptions:** Which businesses are directly affected, through
  what revenue, cost, funding or valuation mechanism, and which related names
  contradict or fall outside that explanation?

A shared theme label does not establish shared economics. Distinguish model
developers, applications, equipment and components when that changes the
explanation. Test adverse and beneficial transmission: a new product that
pressures rivals' pricing could also increase adoption and infrastructure demand.
Neither effect is established by a price screen.

Record event time, first-publication time and quote time separately. Later news
cannot explain an earlier snapshot. If quote timing is unknown, do not claim a
verified before/after reaction. Old news may remain relevant background but a
recap does not make it a fresh trigger. For a historical cutoff, exclude later
knowledge; for a current update, label later developments separately from the
supplied observation.

## Reconcile before writing

Keep a compact internal record per material group/outlier: supplied observation,
earlier backdrop, candidate new driver, affected exposures, mechanism, supporting
source IDs/times, counterevidence and unresolved questions. Reuse existing
quote/news/claim contracts; this is working material, not a required upload format
or a renderer schema.

Test proposed drivers against chronology, business exposure and relative price
behaviour. A confirmed announcement is not automatically a confirmed cause.
Direct and indirect links can coexist: retain a supported secondary influence
without promoting it to the main explanation. Distinguish confidence that a
mechanism exists from evidence that it materially explains this session's move.
Assess its role as a leading explanation, possible contributor, background or
unresolved hypothesis; do not invent numerical attribution. Avoid causal sequence
language such as `spread into hardware` unless evidence supports the sequence.
If a catalyst fits one group more strongly, reflect that difference in the prose.

Check observation scope and causal scope separately. A selected screen supports
claims about its visible names; a broader sector claim needs additional market
evidence. Even when developers and hardware suppliers are both visibly down,
their shared direction does not transfer a developer-specific explanation to
hardware. That extension needs its own evidence of exposure, timing and mechanism.
This applies to the lead and paragraph transitions as well as explicit causal
sentences: a later caveat does not repair an unsupported link implied up front.

Return a complete draft in the selected house format, integrating supplied moves
with the researched explanation, useful exceptions and dated watch points.
Attribute quotes to the supplied snapshot and link news beside supported claims.
A price list or unrelated news digest does not complete the task. When no cause
survives, use `No single catalyst is confirmed` with ranked possibilities and the
specific remaining gap.

Apply the source-verifier's two passes: first verify facts against the actual
inputs and sources, then challenge the explanation using the strongest alternative,
chronology, different exposures and counterevidence. Repeating the same citation
or rerunning the helper is not a second check. Preserve a plausible minor linkage
at its supported weight; lack of proof that it is the main driver does not prove
there is no link.

If either pass finds a material gap that an accessible source could resolve, the
research lead investigates and revises. Recheck affected claims and the final lead
after editing; the editor does not collect new evidence. Do not use `unverified`
as a substitute for feasible research. Disclose access or explicit source/time
limits and keep the conclusion bounded. A populated pack or renderer pass does
not establish research completion.

## Synthetic example: broad weakness and a narrower catalyst

A hypothetical screen shows model developer A down 9%, developer B down 4%, a
board supplier down 5% and an equipment maker down 3%. A supplied headline covers
a rival model launch that morning. Earlier reporting describes infrastructure
funding concerns and hardware weakness over preceding sessions.

Investigate that earlier funding/capex backdrop separately from the launch's
competitive effect on developers. Check for supplier-specific announcements or
changed customer orders. Better model performance could support computing
demand, so the launch alone does not justify an adverse hardware read-through.
A smaller sentiment or valuation linkage may still be plausible if supported;
check it and give it proportionate weight alongside the earlier backdrop. Do
not turn uncertainty about its importance into a claim of no connection. Leave
supplier-specific causes unresolved when the checks cannot establish them.

The lesson is chronology and differentiated exposure, not a standing assumption
that AI weakness comes from funding concerns. Names and moves above are synthetic;
each live request requires fresh evidence.
