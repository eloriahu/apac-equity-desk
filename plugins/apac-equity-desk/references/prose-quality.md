# Causal and prose-quality check

Apply this check to every reader-facing draft after the evidence, fact-check and source-verification work is complete.

## Explain the move

Every material index, sector, theme or security move mentioned in prose needs a driver assessment. Write the chain plainly:

1. what moved, over which session and against which benchmark or peers;
2. the fresh trigger or information the market received;
3. the revenue, cost, cash-flow, risk-premium or valuation mechanism;
4. why the named securities have that exposure and why they moved differently from peers; and
5. the strongest contradiction or the next fact that would confirm or falsify the explanation.

Do not stop at a theme label. "AI and semiconductor stocks led" is incomplete until the note explains why AI and semiconductor stocks rallied and how that impulse reaches the local names. "Risk-on", "policy hopes", "rotation" and "profit-taking" also need a dated trigger and a mechanism.

When the evidence does not establish a cause, write `No single catalyst is confirmed`, then give the ranked plausible factors and the evidence against them. An explicit unresolved assessment is an answer; an unexplained move is not.

## Make it sound written, not generated

- Lead with the useful fact or judgment. Cut throat-clearing and conclusions that merely repeat the note.
- Use flowing paragraphs when a list or extra heading does not improve comprehension.
- Avoid binary setups such as "not X, but Y", dramatic colon reveals, fake-profound endings and generic significance claims.
- Use direct verbs and repeat the clearest term instead of cycling through synonyms.
- Keep bolding for structure, not emphasis inside every sentence.
- Avoid decorative em dashes. Short notes should normally use none beyond the required draft banner.
- Vary sentence length naturally, but do not stack fragments or force every paragraph into the same shape.
- Preserve exact figures, named sources, uncertainty, counterevidence and the writer's useful edge. The prose pass must not make the research sound more certain than the evidence.
