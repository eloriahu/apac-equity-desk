# Desk house style

The house defaults below are derived from the user's close wrap, thematic intraday colour and opening-market snippet. They describe presentation and editorial choices, not verified events or market data. Never reuse sample prices, dates, forecasts, officeholders, tickers or company claims as current facts.

## Voice and evidence

Write for an institutional desk: facts, names, percentage moves, dates and mechanisms. Keep natural variation in paragraph length, dense sector lists, abbreviations and an occasional personal aside. Correct typos without sanding down the writer's voice. Avoid generic summaries and ornamental conclusions.

- Use familiar desk shorthand where clear: NKY, TPX, SBG (after identification), USDJPY, JGB, UST, bps/bp, YoY, ASP and D/G. Do not invent Bloomberg shortcuts or change a listing suffix by guesswork.
- Preserve supplied precision and units. Distinguish an index return, an index contribution and a percentage-point spread; session highs/lows must not become closing moves.
- Attribute broker views, local reporting, specialist data and company statements. Keep disagreement visible. "Some broker expects" needs a named source where available, or a clear attribution gap.
- Distinguish event confirmation from confirmation of a price driver. A company's denial of a rumour is not itself proof of why the stock fell.
- New policy, earnings and valuation changes need an economic mechanism. "More sellers than buyers" can be a supplied joke, never the factual explanation of a price move.
- Link evidence beside the supported sentence or paragraph. Keep source IDs, confidence scores, full breadth/flow tables and audit notes in the data pack unless material to the published narrative.
- Put the as-of time/timezone and draft status in a compact wrapper. Keep data gaps in a separate review note; never fill an empty category with invented prose.
- Preserve user-supplied humour when relevant, as an optional aside near the end. Do not invent a recurring joke, callback or promise to answer something next time.

## Causal completeness and final prose pass

Read `prose-quality.md` before delivery. Every material move needs more than a theme label: give the fresh trigger, the mechanism into earnings or valuation, the reason the named securities carry that exposure and the strongest counterevidence. If the evidence cannot distinguish among drivers, say `No single catalyst is confirmed` and rank the plausible explanations rather than leaving the move unexplained.

The same final pass should make the note sound written by a sharp analyst. Prefer natural paragraphs to decorative formatting, remove canned setups and repeated summaries, and avoid em-dash-heavy rhythm. Keep the numbers, citations, uncertainty and useful detail intact.

## Country close wrap: default end-of-day format

Use flowing paragraphs with a Corporate Headlines section at the end. This is a developed country note, not a six-country paragraph digest. A full regional summary remains an explicit alternative.

1. Open with the index result and divergence: headline index versus broad index, concentration in heavyweight contributors, overnight linkage and the main domestic restraint. "Almost entirely driven by" requires contribution data.
2. Cover local policy and political developments: central-bank expectations, named broker scenarios, fiscal plans and material official remarks. Separate base case from tail risk; attribute each.
3. Give FX and rates their own paragraph when relevant: intraday path versus closing level, curve changes, tenor/yield/bp moves, breakevens, auctions and central-bank operations. Include auction mechanics only if they matter that day.
4. Add macro releases: actual versus consensus, YoY/MoM basis and dated trend comparison when supported.
5. Explain weak sectors with named stock moves, grouping linked sectors compactly. Keep a useful roster of names instead of imposing a "top three" cutoff.
6. Explain outperformers with the same depth, including corporate hooks and relevant exceptions: a semi laggard in a tech rally, or mixed banks despite a rate move.
7. Optionally include the supplied desk aside, then end with **Corporate Headlines** and concise company-led bullets. Omit duplicate detail already covered unless the bullet adds a distinct update.

This order is a useful default, not a requirement to manufacture a policy, rates or macro paragraph every day. Let the dominant story determine emphasis. Breadth, turnover and flows still belong in the underlying pack; bring them into the prose when they explain the move. Upcoming catalysts can sit naturally in the relevant paragraph; do not force a "Tomorrow" conclusion.

Rough guide: 500–900 words for a developed country wrap, with more room on dense sessions and less on quiet ones. The user's requested length overrides the guide.

## Intraday theme colour: default developed commentary

Start with a short, energetic theme headline (uppercase is welcome when warranted), followed by relevant verified desk tickers on separate lines. Then:

1. Describe the move and compare the fresh-news explanation with positioning, valuation or rebound alternatives.
2. Give the fundamental hook: product specification, ASP, volume, capacity, margins or timing of revenue contribution.
3. Use a short "Early demand signals" or "Evidence so far" list when the evidence is mixed. Preserve both supportive and opposing observations, plus any comparison distortions.
4. Use "Implications" for linked stocks in other markets. Explain the specific exposure: lens ASP is different from module content, sensor volumes or currency translation.
5. End with "Things to watch": dated releases, deliveries, monthly sales or measurable tests of the thesis. A suggested proxy such as resale prices is an inference, not proof.

A punchy headline does not justify a causal certainty upgrade. Verify each ticker/company/listing independently; flag inconsistent suffixes rather than silently changing a supplied code. Taiwan themes and Korea/Japan read-through are supported editorially, but this does not add a Taiwan quote feed.

Rough guide: 180–350 words for developed theme colour. Use 90–180 words when the user asks for a flash. The old labelled What/Relative/Catalyst pack is useful for internal review, not the default reader-facing layout.

## Morning snippet: default early-session format

Use a compact country header such as `{JA} JAPAN MORNING` when the country tag is supplied or configured. Usually three paragraphs:

1. The current local tape first: index moves since the relevant reference point, headline/broad-index divergence and the offsetting stock/sector forces.
2. The overnight setup: US equity closes, rates (intraday high versus final level), commodities, FX and the next major domestic/global policy event. Attribute expectations and surveys with dates.
3. Current sector leadership and drag, with the most relevant names and any counterintuitive price reaction.

Default to about 150–250 words, with room for a busy policy morning. Avoid turning this into a 10–15-item watchlist unless requested. If the market has not opened, label the note PRE-OPEN, discuss expectations and do not invent opening performance. Keep the as-of time and the distinction between the prior close and since-open moves.

## Review and research ideas

Desk editing should preserve the chosen format, names, useful density, shorthand, uncertainty and personal voice. Do not automatically compress a developed country wrap to flash length or replace narrative with repeated headings.

Trade ideas remain research scenarios with transmission, horizon, confirmation, invalidation and risks. Research tools are read-only; human approval is required before distribution.
